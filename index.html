Keeping the told joke alive

Chip's Joke Book

Joke telling was a pastime on Southern porches, in Irish pubs, and in English music halls. It is becoming a lost art. This book keeps the jokes worth telling, written the way they are told.

63 jokes. 19 keepers. The standard is number 19: a slow build, real dialogue, and a punchline that gets a laugh.

11. The dog funeral, 8.5 of 10 (keeper)

A man's dog dies. He loved that dog. He goes to the Catholic church and asks the priest to hold a funeral. The priest says, "I'm sorry, we don't bury animals here. Try the little church down the road. They might do it."

The man says, "Thank you, Father. One more question. Do you think a ten thousand dollar donation would be enough for them?"

The priest says, "Wait. Why didn't you tell me the dog was Catholic?"

12. The parrot in the freezer, 9 of 10 (keeper)

A man buys a parrot that swears nonstop. He tries everything. He plays it classical music. He reads it scripture. He shouts at it. Nothing works.

Finally he loses his temper and shoves the parrot in the freezer. The bird squawks and curses for a minute, then goes silent. The man panics and opens the door.

The parrot steps out, shivering, and says, "Sir, I apologize for my language. I've learned my lesson. I'll never swear again." The man is stunned.

The parrot leans in and whispers, "May I ask what the chicken did?"

13. The Pope's driver, 6 of 10

The Pope lands in New York, and a limo picks him up. The Pope says to the driver, "You know, they never let me drive. Could I?" The driver can't say no.

The Pope gets behind the wheel and floors it, ninety miles an hour down the highway. A cop pulls him over, walks up, looks in the window, and goes pale. He radios his chief.

"Chief, I've got a problem. I pulled over somebody very important." "The mayor?" "Bigger." "The governor?" "Bigger." "The President?" "Bigger." "Who's bigger than the President?"

"I don't know, Chief. But the Pope is his driver."

14. The talking dog, 6 of 10

A man sees a sign in a yard: Talking Dog For Sale, ten dollars. He knocks. The owner points to a lab in the backyard.

The man asks the dog, "Can you really talk?" The dog says, "Sure. I was discovered as a puppy. The CIA flew me around the world. I sat in rooms with heads of state, because nobody suspects a dog. I uncovered three spy rings. Then I retired, got married, had a bunch of puppies, and now I volunteer at the VA."

The man turns to the owner. "That's incredible. Why on earth are you selling him for ten dollars?"

The owner says, "Because he's a liar. He never did any of that."

15. Golf with Moses, 7 of 10

Moses, Jesus, and an old man are playing golf. Moses hits into a pond. He raises his club, the water parts, and he chips onto the green. Jesus hits into the same pond. He walks out on the water and chips onto the green.

The old man's drive goes straight toward the pond. A fish jumps up and swallows the ball. An eagle swoops down and grabs the fish. Lightning hits the eagle. The eagle drops the fish onto the green, the fish spits out the ball, and it rolls into the cup. Hole in one.

Jesus turns to the old man and says, "Dad, if you're going to keep doing that, we're not playing with you anymore."

16. Ole's obituary, 7 of 10

Ole dies. Lena calls the newspaper to place the obituary. The clerk asks what she'd like it to say. Lena says, "Ole died."

The clerk says, "Ma'am, it's five words for the same price. You have three more."

Lena thinks for a minute. "Ole died. Boat for sale."

17. The head at the bar, 6 of 10 (Bar jokes)

A man walks into a bar he's never been to. At the far end, sitting on a stool, is a head. Just a head. No body. The bartender sets a whiskey in front of it like it's the most normal thing in the world.

The man stares. The head drinks the whiskey through a straw. And right before his eyes, a neck grows out from under the chin. Then shoulders. Then a whole torso.

The man turns to the bartender. "What on earth is going on?"

The bartender says, "Oh, that's Eddie. He's a regular. Lost everything in a terrible accident years ago. Then one night he ordered our house whiskey, and something grew back. So he keeps coming in."

The torso orders another. A pair of arms grows out. It picks up the glass with its own hands, finishes the drink, and orders a third. Out come the hips. Then the legs. Then the feet. Eddie is whole again, for the first time in years.

The whole bar goes quiet. Eddie stands up, tests his knees, and grins. He says, "Boys, I'm done. I'm going home to my wife." He walks out the front door, steps into the street, and gets hit by a bus. Dead on the spot.

The man sits there in shock. The bartender just shakes his head, wipes down the bar, and says, "He should have quit while he was a head."

18. Farmer Brown's accident (the trooper), 7 of 10 (Farmer Brown)

Farmer Brown is hauling his livestock to market. A cow and a pig ride in the back of his old pickup. Out on the county road, a big delivery truck runs a stop sign and plows right into him. The pickup rolls into the ditch.

Six months later, Farmer Brown sues the trucking company. In court, the company's lawyer stands up, all smiles. "Mr. Brown, isn't it true that right after the accident, the state trooper asked how you were feeling, and you said, quote, 'Never felt better in my life'?"

Farmer Brown says, "Well, yes. But let me tell you what happened." The lawyer says, "Just yes or no, Mr. Brown." The judge leans forward. "I'd like to hear it. Go ahead, Mr. Brown."

"I'm lying in the ditch, hurt bad. My cow is lying next to me, mooing something awful, her leg all busted up. The trooper walks over, takes one look at her, pulls out his pistol, and shoots her right between the eyes.

"Then my pig starts squealing. He's screaming bloody murder, both legs broke. The trooper walks over to the pig, pulls out his pistol, and shoots him too.

"Then he walks over to me, gun still in his hand, and says, 'Sir, how are YOU feeling?'

"And I said, 'Officer, I've never felt better in my life.'"

19. The pig squealed, 8 of 10 (keeper, Farmer Brown)

Chip's own joke. The model for every story joke here.

Farmer Brown was a hard-working man. Dedicated. Always tending to his responsibilities. But once a year, he'd slip out of the house, leave Mrs. Brown behind, drive into town, and go straight to the bar.

On this particular night, he grabbed his coat and headed for the door. Mrs. Brown said, "Where do you think you're going? You're not going anywhere." He ignored her. He climbed in his old pickup and drove into town.

He drank his fair share. And then some. He was well past the point of driving home. It was late. He got behind the wheel anyway and started down the road.

Boom.

He slammed on the brakes. He'd just plowed into a herd of swine. He looked out at the side of the road, and there lay a big hog. Dead.

Farmer Brown sobered up right quick. He knew this was trouble. He didn't want anybody to know. So he hoisted that hog into the back of his flatbed, got behind the wheel, and somehow made it home. He pulled the truck into the barn, shut the doors, and crept into the house.

Mrs. Brown smelled the liquor on his breath. "Where have you been?" He didn't answer. He passed out cold.

At six o'clock in the morning, somebody's pounding on the front door. Mrs. Brown shakes him. "Wake up. Wake up." He keeps snoring. The pounding gets louder. Then a voice: "Police! Open up!"

Now she shakes him hard. "It's the police!"

Farmer Brown goes numb. He pulls on his overalls, pulls himself together, and opens the door.

The officer says, "Farmer Brown, you're under arrest."

"For what?"

"For murder. At midnight last night, there was a wreck out on Highway 12. A hog was killed."

"It wasn't me, I swear. I don't know what you're talking about. I was in bed, sound asleep."

"You're lying. I can see it in your eyes. Where's your truck?"

"My truck?"

Mrs. Brown raises her voice. "Yeah, Earl. Where's your truck?"

"It's parked in the barn. Where I always park it."

"Let's go have a look," says the officer.

They walk out to the barn and swing open the doors. There's the truck. There's blood all over it. And there's a dead hog in the back.

"Farmer Brown, you have the right to remain silent. Anything you say can and will be used against you."

Farmer Brown hangs his head. He's caught. He looks up at the officer and says, "Just tell me one thing. How did you know it was me?"

The officer says, "The pig squealed."

20. The three envelopes, 4 of 10 (Wall Street)

Chip's verdict: banal.

A new CEO takes over a struggling company. On his first morning, the old CEO, the one who just got pushed out, slides three sealed envelopes across the desk, numbered one, two, and three. "This job will knock you down. When it does, open these. In order."

Six months later, sales slump and the board is restless. He opens envelope number one. It says: "Blame your predecessor." He does. The stock bounces.

A year later, margins shrink and a big customer walks. Envelope number two says: "Reorganize." He does. The press calls him a visionary.

Then the third crisis hits, the worst one yet. He locks his office door and with trembling hands opens the last envelope.

It says: "Prepare three envelopes."

21. The monks and the copies, 9 of 10 (keeper)

High in the mountains sits an ancient monastery. For centuries, the monks there have done one sacred job. They copy the holy texts by hand. Day after day. Candle after candle.

A young monk arrives. Bright kid. Eager. After a week, he goes to the old abbot. "Father, may I ask something? We copy every book from the last copy. And that copy was made from the copy before it. If one monk, a hundred years ago, made one small mistake, wouldn't every copy since carry the same mistake?"

The abbot frowns. He strokes his beard. "My son, in six hundred years, no one has ever asked that question."

He takes a lantern and a ring of iron keys. He goes down the stone stairs, deep under the monastery, into the vault where the original scrolls are kept. Nobody has touched them in centuries.

An hour passes. Then two. Then the whole night.

At dawn, the young monk gets worried. He goes down the stairs. In the vault, by the last flicker of the lantern, he finds the old abbot. His robes are torn and his eyes are red. He's banging his forehead against the stone wall and sobbing.

"Father! Father, what's wrong?"

The abbot holds up the ancient scroll with shaking hands and wails, "The word was celebrate!"

22. The green horse, 8 of 10 (Bar jokes)

A skinny little cowboy rides into a rough Texas town. He's dusty and tired after two weeks on the trail. He ties his horse to the rail outside the meanest saloon in the county and walks in for a drink.

He orders a whiskey. Then another. He takes his time, maybe an hour.

He walks back outside, and he stops cold. His horse has been painted green. Bright green, head to tail. Mane, saddle, hooves, everything.

The cowboy is furious. He kicks open the saloon doors, marches to the middle of the room, and shouts, "All right! Which one of you no-good, low-down sons of guns painted my horse?"

The whole saloon goes quiet. The piano stops. Chairs scrape.

In the back corner, a man stands up. And keeps standing up. He's six foot eight and three hundred pounds, with arms like fence posts and a scar across his face. He cracks his knuckles, one at a time, and walks slowly over until he's looking straight down at the little cowboy.

"I did," he growls. "What about it?"

The cowboy looks up. Way up. He swallows hard.

"Well, sir... I just wanted to let you know. The first coat's dry."

23. Farmer Brown and Mrs. Brown at the diner, 9 of 10 (keeper, Farmer Brown)

Farmer Brown and Mrs. Brown drive into town for supper at the diner. They're well into their eighties now. He holds the door for her. She takes his arm. They shuffle to a booth by the window.

Farmer Brown orders one hamburger, one order of fries, and one Coke. That's it.

The food comes. Very carefully, he unwraps the burger and cuts it exactly in half with his pocketknife. He counts out the fries, one for you, one for me, until there are two equal piles. He pours half the Coke into a second cup and slides it across the table to her.

Then he starts eating. Mrs. Brown just sits there with her hands folded.

A young trucker at the counter has been watching the whole thing. It tugs at his heart. He walks over and says, "Excuse me, folks. I don't mean to intrude. But please, let me buy the lady her own meal. It'd be my pleasure."

Farmer Brown smiles. "That's very kind, son. But we're fine. We've been married sixty years. We share everything."

The trucker goes back to the counter. He keeps watching. Farmer Brown keeps eating. Mrs. Brown hasn't touched a bite. Her fries are getting cold. The trucker can't stand it. He goes back over.

"Mrs. Brown, please. Your food's getting cold. Let me get you something hot. Why aren't you eating?"

She looks up at him, sweet as can be, and says, "I'm waiting for the teeth."

24. The genie and the bridge, 8 of 10 (keeper)

A fisherman is out on a California beach at sunrise. He casts his line, and the hook snags on something heavy. He reels it in. It's an old brass lamp, crusted with barnacles.

He rubs off the sand. Poof. A genie appears in a cloud of smoke. "Master, you have freed me. I grant you one wish. Choose carefully."

The fisherman thinks it over. "I've always wanted to go to Hawaii. But I hate flying, and boats make me sick. So I wish for a bridge. From right here, all the way to Hawaii. So I can drive over whenever I want."

The genie's face falls. "A bridge to Hawaii? Do you know what you're asking? Twenty-five hundred miles of open ocean. Pilings down to the bottom of the Pacific. The concrete, the steel, the storms. That's too much, even for me. Pick something else."

The fisherman thinks again. "All right. Here's my wish. I've been married three times. I've never understood a single one of them. I wish I could understand women. Why they cry. What they mean when they say nothing's wrong. What they really want."

The genie stares at him for a long moment. Then he sighs and says, "You want two lanes or four?"

25. Farmer Brown: look, he's moving, 7 of 10 (Farmer Brown)

Farmer Brown and two of his oldest friends, Harold and Walter, are sitting in the back row at a funeral. It's the fourth member of their poker group. After fifty years of Friday nights, there are only three of them.

They watch the line file past the open casket. People are saying the nicest things. "He looks so peaceful." "What a generous man." "He'd give you the shirt off his back."

Afterward, the three of them sit out on the church steps in the sun. They're quiet, thinking about it.

Finally, Harold says, "Fellas, when it's my turn, and I'm lying up there in that box, what do you want people to say about you?"

Walter goes first. "I'd like them to say, he was a fine doctor. Forty years of house calls. And a good family man."

Harold nods. "That's nice. For me, I'd want them to say, he was a devoted husband. A teacher who changed young lives. A man who kept his word."

They both turn to Farmer Brown. He's ninety years old and hasn't said a word all day. "Earl? When you're lying in that casket, and people are filing past, what do you want them to say?"

Farmer Brown thinks about it for a long time. Then he says, "I'd like them to say... look! He's moving!"

26. Farmer Brown's hearing aid, 7 of 10 (Farmer Brown)

Farmer Brown has been deaf as a post for twenty years. The family has given up. At Thanksgiving, the children and grandchildren talk right over him. At Christmas, they talk about him like he's a piece of furniture.

Finally, at ninety-two, he goes to see Doc Henderson. The doc fits him with the newest hearing aid on the market. It's tiny and invisible, tucked down in the ear canal where nobody can see it.

The doc flips it on. Farmer Brown hears the birds outside the window for the first time in two decades. His eyes fill up.

A month later, he comes back for his checkup. The doc tests it. It works perfectly. He hears every word.

Doc Henderson says, "Earl, this is wonderful. Mother and the children must be thrilled that you can hear again."

Farmer Brown leans back in the chair and smiles. "Oh, I haven't told them. I just sit in my recliner and listen. I've changed my will three times."

27. The preacher and the cab driver, 7 of 10

A preacher and a New York cab driver die on the same day. They arrive at the Pearly Gates together.

Saint Peter looks at his book and calls the cab driver forward first. "Welcome, my son." He hands him a robe of pure silk, woven with gold thread. Then a golden staff. Then the keys to a mansion on the hill, with a pool and a view of the whole of heaven. The cab driver walks off grinning, swinging his golden staff.

Now the preacher steps up. He's beaming. He spent fifty years in the pulpit, every Sunday, twice on Easter. He's thinking, if that cabbie got a mansion, wait until they see what I get.

Saint Peter hands him a plain cotton robe, a wooden stick, and the key to a one-room cabin out by the edge of the clouds.

The preacher is stunned. "Saint Peter, there must be some mistake. I'm a man of God. I preached the Gospel for fifty years. That man was a New York cab driver. He ran red lights. He cursed at everybody. How does he get the mansion?"

Saint Peter closes the book and says, "Up here, we go by results. When you preached, people slept. When he drove, people prayed."

28. The gentle mule, 6 of 10

A city fellow buys a little farm in Tennessee. He needs a mule to plow his garden. He drives out to see old Clem, who has one for sale.

Clem walks him out to the pen. "This here's Daisy. Best mule in three counties. Gentle as a lamb. She'll do anything you ask. But you got to ask her nice. Never yell at her. Never raise a hand. Just speak kindly, and she'll plow all day."

The city fellow pays five hundred dollars and leads Daisy home. Next morning, he hitches her to the plow. "Good morning, Daisy. Would you please walk forward?" Nothing. "Daisy, dear, let's get started. Giddy-up, please." Nothing. She just stands there chewing.

He tries for an hour. He tries sweet talk, apples, and sugar cubes. He sings to her. Daisy won't budge an inch. He calls Clem. "That mule you sold me won't move!"

Clem drives over. He looks at Daisy. Then he walks to the woodpile, picks up a two-by-four, and WHACK. He smacks her right between the ears. Then he leans down and whispers, "Daisy, honey, would you please walk on now?" And off she goes, plowing a perfect straight row.

The city fellow is horrified. "Clem! You told me never to raise a hand to her! You said just talk to her nice!"

Clem tosses the board back on the pile. "That's right. But first, you got to get her attention."

29. The smartest man in the world, 8 of 10

A small plane is flying over the Rocky Mountains with four people on board. There's the pilot. There's a famous professor, the one the newspapers call the smartest man in the world. There's an old parish priest. And there's a Boy Scout on his way to a jamboree.

Suddenly, there's a bang. Black smoke. Both engines quit.

The pilot comes running out of the cockpit. "We're going down! There are only three parachutes on this plane. I've got a wife and five kids. I'm sorry." He grabs one, throws open the door, and jumps.

The professor leaps out of his seat. "I am the smartest man in the world! My mind belongs to humanity! The world cannot afford to lose me!" He snatches a pack, and out the door he goes.

Now it's just the old priest and the Boy Scout. The wind is howling through the open door. The plane is diving.

The priest puts a hand on the boy's shoulder. "Son, I've lived a long, full life. I've made my peace with God. You have your whole life ahead of you. Take the last parachute. Go."

The Boy Scout says, "Relax, Father. There are still two parachutes. The smartest man in the world just jumped out with my backpack."

30. Mrs. Brown on the interstate, 7 of 10 (Farmer Brown)
